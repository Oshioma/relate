# Research brief 03 — Mut el-Kharab and the Dakhleh Oasis

**For a researcher with web access. Pasteable as-is.**

This is the highest-value outstanding task on the Set timeline, and the reason
is structural rather than decorative.

The dataset currently tells Set's decline as one long, uneven, national change.
It has a record called `set-survives-in-the-oases` whose entire evidential
content is *"the temple site at Mut el-Kharab has been excavated as a temple of
Seth, and the oasis material is regularly cited for exactly this point."* It
carries **one** date claim, that claim is **positionless**, its source is
**null**, and the record says of itself: *"Nothing here has been read… this is a
signpost, and it is labelled as one."*

That signpost is load-bearing. If Set's cult was proscribed in parts of Egypt
while continuing at Dakhleh, then "Egypt turned against Set" is the wrong shape
for the evidence, and the timeline needs two geographic lanes rather than one
national story. Everything below is aimed at putting objects behind that.

---

## The standard

- **Quote verbatim, with page and figure references.** Do not paraphrase and
  present it as the excavator's words.
- **Never reconstruct an object number, transliteration or figure reference from
  memory.** Quote it or write `NOT FOUND`.
- **If you cannot open something, write `NOT FOUND`** rather than answering from
  general knowledge.
- **Give the URL you actually opened** for every claim.
- **Separate the object from the publication about it.** The commonest way to
  lose a provenance is to let the edition stand in for the artefact.
- **Report damage, illegibility and the excavators' own hedges.** Those are
  wanted, not smoothed over.

A long `NOT FOUND` list is a good result. A plausible excavation number that
turns out wrong is the worst possible one, because it looks checkable.

## The object records — the core of the task

**Do not return one general summary of the site.** Return one block per object.
The dataset's rule is that a statement must be walkable back to a thing, and a
paragraph saying "Set was worshipped here" is precisely what it already has.

```
OBJECT:              <name>
OBJECT NUMBER:       <excavation / registration number, or NOT FOUND>
CURRENT LOCATION:    <museum, magazine, or in situ>
MATERIAL & SIZE:
FINDSPOT:            <trench, area, context>
STRATIGRAPHY:        <phase, and HOW that phase was dated>
DATE:                <and the basis — stratigraphic, epigraphic, stylistic, royal name>
DESCRIPTION:         <what is physically there>
INSCRIPTION:         <what the text says is present; note damage>
TRANSLITERATION:     <published, or NOT FOUND>
TRANSLATION:         <published, with translator and page>
SET REFERENCES:      <how Seth is named; which epithets>
SET DETERMINATIVE:   <is the Set animal present, absent, altered, erased? Describe
                      exactly what is visible>
PUBLICATION:         <author, year, PAGE, FIGURE/PLATE NUMBER>
EXCAVATOR'S READING: <what they themselves conclude, quoted>
IMAGE:               <see the image block below>
URLS OPENED:
```

### The objects wanted

1. **Earliest evidence for Seth at Dakhleh.** What is the oldest thing at the
   site that names or depicts him, and how is it dated?
2. **New Kingdom temple activity.** What establishes a temple here in the New
   Kingdom rather than later?
3. **The Ramesside stela for Seth** — the one Hope & Kaper publish as a new
   stela. Full object block. This is a priority.
4. **Seth with Nephthys.** Use the excavators' own description. **Do not
   reconstruct missing iconography from mythology** — if the relief is damaged,
   say what survives.
5. **Seth and Amun at the site.** What is the relationship in the material, in
   the excavators' words?
6. **The Greater Dakhleh Stela.** Full object block, and the fullest possible
   treatment of its Set references and its **Set-animal determinative**.
7. **The Smaller Dakhleh Stela.** Full object block, for comparison with the
   above.
8. **Altered or erased Seth inscriptions.** Each one separately, with what was
   altered and how it is dated.
9. **Temple blocks, Seth figures, and excavation-context photographs.**

## The determinative question — handle with care

This is the part most likely to be reported badly elsewhere, so it gets its own
instruction.

If the Greater and Smaller Dakhleh Stelae write Seth's name differently — if the
Set animal is present in one and absent, replaced or altered in the other —
report **exactly what is visible on each**, with figure references, and then
report **what the excavators conclude and why**.

**Do not call the change demonisation.** That is the conclusion under test, and
if the brief supplies it the answer is worthless. The questions are:

- What changed?
- When, and how is that dated?
- Why do the excavators think it changed?
- Do they consider alternatives — orthographic convention, scribal habit,
  damage, later recutting?

If they disagree with each other, send both positions.

## The proscription, modelled geographically

From **Hope & Warfe, *The Proscription of Seth Revisited***.

The dataset must not produce a national date called "Set banned". So:

- What does the paper argue, in its own words? Quote the conclusion.
- Does it model the proscription **by region** — Thebes, Memphis, the Delta,
  Dakhleh, the other western oases? If so, give the evidence per region and the
  dating per region.
- What does it say about **why** the Dakhleh evidence differs?
- What does it say the evidence **cannot** establish?

The aim is to be able to show different regional trajectories side by side, so
per-region dated evidence is worth more than any general statement.

## Late survival, period by period

The record currently says the cult continued "later than in much of the Nile
Valley" and enters no span, because none was established. To fix that, I need
dated evidence, not continuity.

For **each** of: Third Intermediate Period · Late Period · Ptolemaic · Roman ·
Christianisation —

```
PERIOD:
PHYSICAL OR TEXTUAL EVIDENCE:  <named objects, with numbers and publication>
WHAT IT SHOWS:                 <cult activity? construction? mere occupation?>
DATING BASIS:
EXCAVATORS' READING:
NOT FOUND:                     <where a period has no evidence, say so plainly>
```

**Two cautions, and they matter more than the table.**

**Do not infer continuous worship from a standing temple.** A building surviving
is not a cult continuing. If a period has no evidence of cult activity, that
gap is a finding and I want it recorded as one — the dataset has a record whose
entire job is to stop gaps being quietly filled.

**Distinguish occupation from cult.** Pottery in a stratum shows people were
there. It does not show anyone was making offerings to Seth.

## Images

Priority: the temple; the Ramesside Seth stela; Seth with Nephthys; the Greater
Dakhleh Stela; the Smaller Dakhleh Stela; Set-animal determinatives in detail;
altered Seth inscriptions; temple blocks; Seth figures; excavation context.

**Do not substitute generic Set imagery.** A photograph of a Set statue from a
museum attached to a record about a Dakhleh stela is worse than no photograph,
and this dataset has a test that would eventually catch it.

Excavation-report figures are usually under copyright. That is fine and expected
— **give the figure reference so a reader can find it**, and mark whether the
image itself is reusable:

```
IMAGE OF:            <which object>
FIGURE REFERENCE:    <publication, figure/plate number, page>
IMAGE URL:           <if one is openly available>
FILE PAGE URL:       <the description page, not the image file>
HOLDING INSTITUTION:
LICENCE:             <verbatim, from the file's own page — or "in copyright,
                      reference only">
CREATOR:             <photographer / draughtsman>
OBJECT DATE:         <when the depicted thing was made>
PHOTOGRAPH DATE:     <when the photograph was taken>
IMAGE TYPE:          photograph / line drawing / plan / section / squeeze
WHAT THE VIEWER IS SEEING:  <plain description>
```

A **line drawing** of an inscription is often more useful here than a
photograph, because the determinative question turns on legibility. Send both
where both exist, and label which is which.

## Five questions to answer explicitly

Each with a citation. **A "no" or "not established" is as valuable as a "yes"** —
it tells me to change the record rather than keep it.

**Q1. Is "Seth, Lord of the Oasis" an attested epithet, in those words?**
Give the Egyptian, a published transliteration, the object it appears on, and
the publication. The dataset currently uses the phrase on the strength of a
paper title.

**Q2. How late is the latest securely dated evidence of Seth cult at Dakhleh?**
Name the object, its date, and the dating basis. This is the single figure that
would turn the record's positionless claim into a real span.

**Q3. Does the Dakhleh evidence actually overlap in date with hostility to Seth
in the Nile Valley?** The whole argument depends on the two being contemporary.
Is that demonstrated, or assumed? If the Dakhleh material is simply *earlier*
than the valley's turn, the record needs rewriting.

**Q4. Was there a break?** Is the Dakhleh evidence continuous, or is it clusters
with gaps? If there are gaps, where are they and how long?

**Q5. What do the excavators say they cannot establish?** The limits section of
a report is usually the most useful page in it, and almost never gets quoted.

---

## What not to do

- Do not design timeline records or decide classifications — that needs the
  codebase open.
- Do not reproduce a copyrighted excavation report in bulk. Quote what is needed
  and cite page and figure.
- Do not resolve a disagreement between excavators. Send both.
- Do not fill an object number, figure reference or transliteration by
  inference. `NOT FOUND` is better.
- Do not upgrade "a temple stood here" into "a cult continued here". That step
  is the one this whole brief exists to prevent.
