# Research batch 09: a line for every day from five more traditions

Oshi asked for at least 8 interpretations a day and chose Maya, Akan, Igbo,
Chinese and Hindu (2 November 2026). With this batch the next 30 days average
about 10 lines from 8 traditions a day.

## What each tradition adds, and how its date is worked out
- **Maya tzolkʼin** (20 day names): the meaning of each day and the 1854
  highland Guatemalan reading of it (Hernández Spina, via Weeks, Sachse and
  Prager 2009, as summarised by Wikipedia), e.g. Tzikin "the most excellent
  day". Counted from 21 Dec 2012 = 4 Ajaw (GMT correlation 584,283), which the
  living highland count agrees with. Checked: 16 May 2026 = 10 Ix 7 Sip (FAMSI).
  Plus the five Wayebʼ days of the 365-day haabʼ.
- **Akan**: the six-day week (nnanson: Fo, Nwuna, Nkyi, Kuru, Kwa, Mono) and the
  seven-day week (Kwasiada…Memeneda, with day names), and the other three dabɔne
  of the 42-day cycle (Fɔdwo, Awukudae, Fofie: no farming; no funerals on an
  adae). Counted from the published Akwasidae of 1 Feb 2026 (Kuru on a Sunday);
  Bartle's 1978 dates fall on the same cycle.
- **Igbo**: the four market days, now dated from the Nri anchor (18 Feb 2012
  "an Nkwọ day", Ịgụ Arọ), labelled as the Nri count: other towns differ.
- **Chinese twelve day officers** (建除): Huainanzi grouping, one rule each from
  a Dunhuang almanac of 924 CE, the Song official calendar's unused days, and
  the Jian day's modern list. Dated by the Qing rule (day branch against the
  solar-term month, repeat at each jie) in China time. Checked: 2 Nov 2026 is
  庚辰, 月破 (破 day).
- **Hindu tithi** (30): the tithi at sunrise in Ujjain from true Sun and Moon
  positions; Ekādaśī fasts, Daśamī and Dvādaśī around them, Pradoṣa,
  Saṅkaṣṭī Caturthī, Amāvasyā ancestor rites, Pūrṇimā, and which yearly
  festivals fall on each tithi. Checked: 2 Nov 2026 is Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī.
