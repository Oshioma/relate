# Research batch 08: Babylonian moon days

Written for the daily knowledge engine (Oshi asked for "any info about ancient
Babylonian moon day knowledge"). Every record cites the page it comes from;
quotations are the sources' own translations.

## Sources (3)
- **University of Hamburg, CSMC, "Every day has its own activity"** (Mesopotamian
  Bulletins, 26 February 2025): hemerologies and menologies, with quotations
  from the tablets. The source of every month-specific day below.
- **Wikipedia, "Babylonian calendar"**: month names and their Sumerian meanings,
  the month starting at the first crescent, the 7th/14th/21st/28th days and
  their gods, šapattu at the full Moon.
- **Jewish Encyclopedia (1906), "Sabbath"**: the 7th, 14th, 19th, 21st and 28th of
  Ulūlu (Elul) as days of abstinence, and why the 19th (the 49th day from the
  previous month's start). Older scholarship; labelled as such.

## Records
- Culture `middle-east.babylonian`, system `middle-east.babylonian.lunisolar`.
- 13 months (12 plus the inserted Addaru II), with the Sumerian meanings.
- 7 days of every month: first crescent (1), the four phase days (7, 14, 21, 28),
  šapattu (15), day 19.
- 29 days of particular months (observances with `month_key`), each with a
  practice: no oath on 15 Nisānu, marrying on 3 Nisānu and 8 Āru (not 7 Āru), no
  boat on 23 Simanu, no road on 4 Dumuzu, foods to avoid in Nisānu, Tišritum and
  Araḫsamnu, Nisānu's dangerous days for the sick, Ulūlu's days of abstinence.

## Dates
Placed by the illustrative "Babylonian month" profile (mapping.ts): day 1 is the
first evening the Moon is a day old in Babylon time, and Nisānu is the month
that begins nearest the March equinox. Month-specific days fall once a year, in
their own month. Every row says "Illustrative": these are where the days would
fall if the calendar were kept today, not ancient listings.

## Modern notes
The four phase days carry one modern suggestion ("treat it as a quiet day"),
marked as modern. Nothing else is modern.
