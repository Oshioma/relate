# Research batch 11: a Babylonian line for every day of the Moon's month

Oshi asked (6 November 2026) for Babylonian knowledge on every day of the
lunar month. Batch 08 covered days 1, 7, 14, 15, 19, 21 and 28 plus scattered
month-specific rules. This batch adds all 30 days from one complete source:

- **The hemerology of the second (intercalary) Ulūlu**, a tablet from
  Ashurbanipal's library (W.A.I. IV 32–33), in A. H. Sayce's translation
  (Hibbert Lectures 1887, pp. 70–76, public domain). For each day it gives
  the god, whether the day is lucky, and the king's offering; on the 7th,
  14th, 19th, 21st and 28th it lists the restrictions (no cooked meat, no
  change of clothes, no chariot, no decrees, no medicine, no cursing).

New `lunar_days` rows for the 23 days batch 08 lacked, and one
recommendation per day (all 30), attached to the day so it shows every month.

## Caveats, shown on every line
- One tablet, for one (extra) month, addressed to the king; other months'
  tablets differ in detail.
- Sayce's "Sabbath" and "fast-day" are 1887 renderings; modern scholars read
  the restricted days as "evil days" (ūmu lemnu). Gods' names are given in
  modern forms with Sayce's in brackets (Nebo = Nabû, Adar = Ninurta,
  Rimmon = Adad, Bel and Beltis = Bēl and Bēltiya).
- Modern dates come from the existing illustrative Babylonian month model.

The fuller modern editions (Labat 1965; Livingstone 2013, *Hemerologies of
Assyrian and Babylonian Scholars*) are in copyright and weren't reachable; a
later batch could add their month-by-month differences as short summaries.
