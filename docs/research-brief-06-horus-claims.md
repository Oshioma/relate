# Research brief 06 — Four claims about Horus

**For a researcher with web access. Pasteable as-is.**

Investigate four claims frequently compared with Jesus:

1. Horus was born of a virgin.
2. Horus was born on December 25.
3. Horus had twelve disciples.
4. Horus was crucified.

**Do not begin by assuming these are true or false.** The purpose is to
determine where each claim originated, what ancient evidence lies behind it,
whether a genuine Egyptian tradition has been reinterpreted into Christian
terminology, and when the modern formulation first appears.

A preliminary check has already established the shape of the answer, and it is
worth stating so you can test it rather than rediscover it: **the virgin-birth
claim is different in kind from the other three.** Egyptian tradition really
does contain an extraordinary conception — Isis conceives Horus from Osiris
*after Osiris has been killed*. That is an authentic ancient tradition and it is
not straightforwardly a virginal conception in the Christian sense. For the
other three, no Egyptian primary source has been located. Your job is to test
both halves of that and to establish the chains.

---

## The standard

- **Quote verbatim, with page, line or catalogue references.**
- **Never reconstruct a citation, accession number or passage from memory.**
  Quote it or write `NOT FOUND`.
- **Give the URL you actually opened** for every claim.
- **A negative is a finding.** "Searched X, Y and Z; not found" is a result, and
  it must name where you searched.
- **Follow citations backwards until they stop.** If a chain ends in a source
  that cannot be located, mark it **BROKEN** rather than repeating it.
- **Check both sides.** Do not rely on Christian apologetic sites to reject a
  parallel, or on mythicist sites to establish one. If an apologist says "there
  is no Egyptian source", verify independently. If an alternative writer says
  "Egyptian texts clearly show this", locate the text.

---

## The six categories — do not collapse them

Every claim is separated into:

| | |
|---|---|
| **A** | Ancient primary evidence |
| **B** | Later Greco-Roman evidence |
| **C** | Early Egyptological interpretation |
| **D** | 19th/20th-century alternative interpretation |
| **E** | Modern popular claim |
| **F** | Current scholarly interpretation |

**A claim can be historically important even when it is incorrect.** If Gerald
Massey claimed in 1883 that Horus had twelve followers, *that claim is itself a
dated historical event* and deserves its own record, whether or not any Egyptian
text supports it.

Likewise, where an Egyptian scene was later read as a crucifixion, preserve
**both** what the object actually depicts **and** how later writers read it.
Do not silently drop a controversial interpretation.

---

## HORUS IS NOT ONE PERSON

The single most important methodological point, and the one most likely to
wreck the results if ignored.

Egyptian religion ran for thousands of years and contained multiple Horus
deities, regional traditions and evolving theologies. **A tradition about Horus
the Elder must not be attributed to Horus son of Isis.**

Distinguish throughout: **Horus the Younger** · **Harsiese / Hor-sa-Iset**
("Horus son of Isis") · **Harpocrates** · **Horus the Elder / Haroeris** ·
**Horus of Behdet** · other relevant forms.

Plutarch himself preserves traditions involving different forms of Horus, which
is exactly why "Horus" cannot be treated as one biography.

**And: OSIRIS ≠ HORUS.** If a death-and-resurrection tradition actually concerns
Osiris, record it under Osiris. Do *not* transfer it to Horus. But **do** record
where later writers deliberately merged them into an Osiris–Horus or solar
theological reading — that merging is itself a datable interpretive act.

## 1 — VIRGIN BIRTH

Find the earliest Egyptian evidence for Isis as Horus's mother. Then find the
earliest surviving account of the conception.

Investigate: Osiris's murder · Isis recovering the body · reconstruction or
reanimation of Osiris · Isis in kite form · Isis hovering over Osiris ·
restoration of Osiris's phallus · the conception · **whether intercourse is
explicitly represented or implied** · whether magical-conception terminology
appears · **whether Isis is ever explicitly called a virgin in connection with
conceiving Horus**.

**Do not reduce this to "Osiris was the father, therefore not a virgin."**
Ancient concepts of miraculous conception need not map onto modern biological
categories. Determine precisely what the sources say.

Search for Egyptian terms and titles applied to Isis that have been translated
or interpreted as *virgin*, *maiden*, *young woman*, *great virgin*, *divine
virgin*, *unmarried*, *pure* — and determine whether any of them actually refers
to the conception of Horus.

**The Luxor Temple / Amenhotep III birth scenes** are separately cited as an
Egyptian "virgin annunciation". Establish: what the relief actually depicts ·
its date · who is depicted · the inscriptions · whether Horus is involved ·
whether Isis is involved · whether a divine conception is depicted · whether any
genuine similarity with Christian Annunciation imagery exists · **and where the
"Egyptian virgin birth" reading originated**.

---

## 2 — DECEMBER 25

**Do not simply answer that Egyptians did not use the Julian calendar.** Find
the actual festival and birth traditions.

Investigate: Egyptian birthdays of gods · the epagomenal days · the births of
Osiris, Horus the Elder, Set, Isis and Nephthys · Horus the Younger · Khoiak ·
winter-solstice celebrations · solar Horus traditions · the rebirth of the sun ·
Harpocrates · Roman-Egyptian festivals · Alexandrian religious calendars ·
Plutarch on divine birthdays.

**Could any Horus festival fall near the winter solstice in any period?** If so,
explain how a later writer could have converted that into "December 25".

Then trace the **earliest identifiable publication** explicitly stating "Horus
was born December 25": author, work, year, page if possible, and **what source
that author claimed to rely on**.

---

## 3 — TWELVE FOLLOWERS

Search broadly for any ancient tradition later writers might have transformed
into "twelve disciples":

Shemsu-Hor / Followers of Horus · Companions of Horus · Followers of the King ·
Sons of Horus · gods accompanying Horus · Horus's allies against Set · celestial
followers · solar barque traditions · the twelve hours of day and night · the
twelve divisions of the Duat · zodiacal readings · groups of followers in temple
scenes.

**"Followers" and "disciples" are not automatically equivalent.** Determine
whether any Egyptian text or image gives Horus a group of **exactly twelve**
beings that could reasonably have been transformed into "twelve disciples".

Then trace the modern claim through: Gerald Massey · Kersey Graves · Godfrey
Higgins · Albert Churchward · D. M. Murdock / Acharya S · the Zeitgeist
Sourcebook · *Zeitgeist: The Movie*. Identify the **earliest** explicit
formulation you can find.

---

## 4 — CRUCIFIXION

Investigate especially carefully, and **do not search only for literal Roman
crucifixion**. Separate:

**A** execution by crucifixion · **B** binding to a tree or pole · **C** arms
extended in a cross-like pose · **D** cross symbolism · **E** ankh symbolism ·
**F** celestial or astronomical "crucifixion" · **G** the death of Horus ·
**H** the death of Osiris · **I** later allegorical readings.

For **every** image cited by writers claiming Horus was crucified, identify:
museum · accession number · approximate date · provenance · deity depicted ·
the original Egyptian interpretation · the modern alternative interpretation.

Find the **earliest known source** for each of these separately:

- "Horus was crucified between two thieves"
- "Horus was crucified on a cross"
- "Horus was crucified in the heavens"

Then work backwards through that author's citations.

**Gerald Massey's astronomical interpretation is particularly important.**
Explain what Massey *actually argued*, from his own text, rather than from
summaries of him. Note also that Murdock explicitly defended a
metaphorical/celestial reading of "crucifixion" — which is importantly different
from finding an Egyptian text describing an execution, and the difference must
survive into the record.

## Output format

The database has a **claim genealogy** structure built for exactly this, so
please return results in its shape. One block per claim:

```
CLAIM:               <in the words people actually use for it>
VERDICT:             <one of the six below>
VERDICT EVIDENCE:    <why that verdict, and what it does NOT say>
WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS ASSESSMENT:
                     <the specific evidence that would upgrade or overturn it>
```

Verdicts — **not true/false, and the six exist because two cannot hold this**:

`DIRECTLY ATTESTED` · `PARTIALLY ATTESTED` · `RELATED ANCIENT TRADITION EXISTS` ·
`LATER INTERPRETATION` · `NO PRIMARY EVIDENCE LOCATED` · `SOURCE DISPUTED`

Then the chain, **oldest first**, one block per link:

```
STAGE:               ancient_primary | later_antiquity | early_scholarship |
                     alternative_interpretation | popular_claim | current_scholarship
WHO:                 <person or work>
YEAR:
REFERENCE:           <volume, page, line, figure>
SAYS:                <what THIS link actually states, quoted — not what the
                      next link reports it as saying>
                     <or: NOT READ, and why>
ADDS:                <what this link contributed that its predecessor did not
                      contain — the most important field here>
CITATION STATUS:     verified | unverified | broken | misattributed | no_citation_given
```

**`ADDS` is the field that matters most.** These claims rarely arrive whole:
one writer supplies an object, the next supplies a reading, the next drops the
hedge, the next adds a detail nobody before them had. Recording what each link
contributed is what turns a bibliography into an explanation.

**`CITATION STATUS: broken`** means the citation leads to a source that cannot
be located, or to a passage that is not there. Mark it — everything downstream
of a broken link rests on nothing, and saying where the chain stops is more
useful than any verdict.

Finish with the comparison matrix:

```
CLAIM | EARLIEST ANCIENT EVIDENCE | EARLIEST MODERN CLAIM | PRIMARY SOURCE? |
WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY SAYS | INTERPRETATION HISTORY
```

And a `NOT FOUND` list, plus `SOURCES I COULD NOT OPEN`.

## What not to do

- Do not assume the genealogy in advance. The chain
  *ancient source → early translation → 19th-century reading → Massey →
  20th-century literature → Acharya S → Zeitgeist → internet* is a **hypothesis
  to test**, not a template to fill.
- Do not use simplistic TRUE/FALSE labels.
- Do not silently remove a controversial interpretation.
- Do not transfer an Osiris tradition to Horus, or a Horus the Elder tradition
  to Horus son of Isis.
- Do not design timeline records or assign classifications — that needs the
  codebase open.

**The goal is not to prove or disprove that Jesus was "copied from Horus".** It
is to reconstruct the evidence chain so a reader can see: what the Egyptians
actually said → what later writers thought it meant → how the comparison
developed.
