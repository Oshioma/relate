## The standard

Unchanged since brief 01, and it has now produced four usable rounds — the
400-Year Stela, Mut el-Kharab, the Contendings, and the four Horus claims.

- **Quote verbatim, with page, line or catalogue references.**
- **Never reconstruct a transliteration, accession number or figure reference
  from memory.** Quote it or write `NOT FOUND`.
- **If you cannot open something, write `NOT FOUND`** rather than answering from
  general knowledge.
- **Give the URL you actually opened** for every claim.
- **Keep the lacunae and the hedges.** An editor's own statement of what they
  cannot establish has been the most valuable material in every round so far.
- **Refusing is correct when the source does not support the question.** Round 04
  was asked for a regional model its authors had not drawn, and correctly
  reported that they had not drawn one. Do that wherever it applies.
- **A negative is a finding.** Round 05 was asked whether a text calls Seth evil,
  searched, and reported that it does not. That answer changed a record.

## What this collection is for, and the trap it must avoid

The question is: **how old is the idea of a spiritual power rising through the
human body, and why do serpents, staffs, trees, fire and ascent keep appearing
in sacred traditions worldwide?**

The trap is obvious and the whole collection is built against it. Serpent
imagery is close to universal. Vertical axis imagery is close to universal. It
is therefore very easy to assemble a hundred objects that "look remarkably
similar" and let the accumulation do the arguing.

So the collection will classify every record as one of these — **and these are
not confidence scores, and must never be given numbers:**

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **EXPLICIT KUNDALINI** | The source itself concerns Kuṇḍalinī, or an unmistakably identified Kundalini practice. |
| **HISTORICAL PRECURSOR** | An earlier idea *within the relevant Indian tradition* that demonstrably contributed to, or closely precedes, later subtle-body systems. |
| **RELATED TRADITION** | A historically connected Tantric/yogic/subtle-body system, not necessarily called Kundalini. |
| **CROSS-CULTURAL PARALLEL** | A real structural or visual similarity with **no demonstrated historical connection**. |
| **SPECULATIVE / ESOTERIC** | An interesting comparison requiring assumptions the surviving evidence does not establish. |

**Your job is not to assign these labels** — that needs the codebase open. Your
job is to supply the evidence that lets them be assigned, and in particular to
answer, for every item: **is there any evidence of historical transmission, or
only resemblance?** Those are different questions and the collection keeps them
in different fields.

## PART A — The Indus Valley

Roughly 3300–1300 BCE; Mature Harappan c. 2600–1900 BCE.

**The governing fact: the Indus script is undeciphered.** No record in this
collection may say "the Indus people practised Kundalini yoga", or anything that
implies it. What is wanted is: *what is physically visible on the object*, and
then, separately and attributed, *what people have said it means*.

### A1. The Pashupati seal — priority

The single most-argued object in this section.

```
OBJECT:              <name as the holding institution gives it>
SEAL NUMBER:         M-304? — verify; give the Mahadevan/CISI reference
CURRENT LOCATION:    <museum, and accession number>
EXCAVATION:          <site, trench, level, excavator, season>
MATERIAL & SIZE:
DATE:                <and the basis for it — stratigraphic? stylistic?>
WHAT IS PHYSICALLY VISIBLE: <describe only what is on the seal: the figure,
                      posture, headdress, surrounding animals, any signs>
PUBLICATION:         <with figure/plate number>
URLS OPENED:
```

Then, **separately**, one block per interpretation:

```
INTERPRETATION:      <what it is claimed to be>
WHOSE:               <named scholar>
YEAR:
ARGUMENT:            <their reasoning, quoted where possible>
STATUS:              <current / minority / superseded — as the literature treats it>
PUBLISHED IN:
```

Wanted at minimum: **Marshall's original proto-Śiva identification**; the
**yogic-posture reading**; and the **criticisms of both** — Sullivan, Srinivasan
and others have argued against the proto-Śiva reading, and their objections are
as wanted as the proposal. Also: does anyone argue the posture is *not* yogic,
and on what grounds (seating conventions, bovine features, comparative
iconography)?

**Specific question:** is the seated figure's posture attested elsewhere in
Indus material, and is it attested in *non*-religious contexts?

### A2–A7. The rest of the section

For each, the same split — *what is visible*, then *attributed interpretations*:

- **Indus serpent imagery.** Which seals or tablets, with numbers. Is a serpent
  ever shown with a seated figure? Give the object.
- **The triangular Mohenjo-daro tablets/amulets** sometimes cited here.
- **Pipal / sacred tree imagery**, and any human–tree–serpent combinations. Give
  object numbers; this motif is heavily reproduced without provenance.
- **Fertility or goddess interpretations**, only where genuinely documented, with
  whose interpretation it is.
- **The Great Bath** — what it physically is, and what ritual-bathing claims
  rest on.

**Question for the whole section:** what is the strongest *scholarly* statement
that Indus material connects to later Indian religious practice — and what is
the strongest statement against? Quote both.

## PART B — Mesopotamia

### B1. The libation vase of Gudea — priority

This gets its own major record, and the brief's own instruction is that the
comparison with Iḍā–Suṣumṇā–Piṅgalā must be labelled a **cross-cultural visual
comparison**, with an explicit statement that there is no demonstrated evidence
the image represents Kundalini or human spinal energy.

```
OBJECT:              Libation vase / vase of Gudea
MUSEUM:              <and accession number — Louvre?>
EXCAVATION:          <site, findspot, excavator>
DATE:                <c. 2100 BCE? give the basis and the scholarly range>
MATERIAL & SIZE:
INSCRIPTION:         <what it says — quote a published translation>
DEDICATION:          <to which deity, in the inscription's own words>
WHAT IS DEPICTED:    <describe precisely: how many serpents, how they are
                      arranged, what the central element actually is, what else
                      is on the vase>
PUBLICATION:
IMAGES:              <multiple angles/details if available, per the image block>
URLS OPENED:
```

**Three specific questions:**

1. **What is the central vertical element?** It is routinely called a "staff" or
   "axis". What do Assyriologists call it, and is that reading secure?
2. **Is the entwined-serpent motif attested elsewhere in Mesopotamian art**, and
   if so where and when? A motif with a local history is a different thing from
   an isolated image.
3. **What is Ningishzida's actual role** in Mesopotamian religion, in the
   sources? Chthonic, healing, vegetation, kingship — quote a specialist.

### B2–B6

- **Ningishzida** more broadly; serpent deities; entwined serpents elsewhere.
- **Mesopotamian sacred trees and "Tree of Life" imagery** — and critically, is
  "Tree of Life" the Mesopotamian name or a modern scholarly label? That
  question matters to a whole section of this collection.
- **Healing, fertility, regeneration and underworld** serpent associations.
- **The Gilgamesh serpent episode** — the snake that takes the plant of
  rejuvenation. Give tablet and line references from a standard edition.

## PART C — The transmission question

This runs across the whole collection and deserves its own answer.

**Was there plausible historical contact** between Mesopotamia, the Indus,
Iran, Greece and India in the relevant periods — trade routes, Achaemenid
administration, Hellenistic contact, the Indo-Greek kingdoms, Roman-era Indian
Ocean trade?

Two separate questions, and please answer them separately:

1. **Was contact possible?** What does the evidence show about routes, goods,
   people and dates?
2. **Is there evidence that these specific motifs or ideas travelled?** Named
   scholars arguing for transmission, named scholars arguing against, and what
   each rests on.

**Do not let the first answer stand in for the second.** "Trade routes existed"
is not evidence that a subtle-body doctrine moved along them, and the collection
needs to be able to show a reader exactly that gap.

### What is still wanted, and it is narrow

**D1. `pashupati-seal` — a photograph AND the standard drawing, as two entries.**
The seal is small and worn; the drawing is how most people have actually seen it,
and the difference between the object and the drawing is part of the record's
subject. If what is there now is one and not the other, say which.

**D2. `gudea-vase-entwined-serpents` — the Louvre's own catalogue record for
AO 190**, as `originalSourceUrl`, plus the accession details and whatever the
Louvre states about the object. The record currently admits it does not know the
vase's accession number, excavation context, or what its inscription says.

**D3. Is the entwined-serpent motif attested elsewhere in Mesopotamian art?**
This is an image question and a substantive one: **a motif with a local history
in Mesopotamian art is a very different thing from an isolated image**, and the
record says so. Any other securely provenanced examples, with their objects.

**D4. Anything labelled "ancient Kundalini" that is in fact modern.** These
circulate heavily. A documented example — with what it actually is and when it
was made — is worth more here than another genuine ancient object.

For anything you do send, use the full field list from brief 07. The older field
list is kept below because it is still correct, and because it names the traps:

```

ORIGINAL FILE URL:   <full size, no width parameter>

INSTITUTION:

                      filed under>

```

**Report copyright honestly.** "In copyright, reference only, figure N of
publication X" is a useful answer. A guessed licence is not.

**Actively look for provenance problems.** Real examples from earlier rounds: a
show bill dated 1886 filed under a man who died in 1806; a 2011 street-art
photograph whose filename began "Cardiff1869"; an "own work" credit dated to the
year the subject died; and a widely reused "Horus spearing Set" image that turned
out to be a modern artwork made in 2025. **A category is a filing decision, not
a provenance.**

This section is especially exposed: images labelled "ancient Kundalini" circulate
heavily and are frequently modern. **Never accept a caption as identification.**

## PART E — Six questions

**Q1.** Is there any *securely dated* Indus object that shows a serpent together
with a seated human figure? If yes, give it. If no, say so — that absence is
load-bearing for the whole Indus section.

**Q2.** What does the Gudea vase inscription actually say? Does it mention
serpents, ascent, healing, or the body at all?

**Q3.** Is "Tree of Life" an ancient Near Eastern term or a modern label applied
by scholars? Who introduced it, and when?

**Q4.** What is the earliest *securely dated* occurrence of the word
**kuṇḍalinī** in a datable text? Name the text, the passage, the proposed date
and the scholar who proposed it. (This is the anchor for the whole collection and
will be briefed in full later — an early answer here would be valuable.)

**Q5.** Do any specialists in Indus, Mesopotamian or Egyptian material
*themselves* draw a comparison with Indian subtle-body systems? Not popular
writers — specialists. If none do, that is an important finding and should be
stated.

**Q6.** What do the excavators and editors say they **cannot** establish? Their
stated limits, quoted.

## Return format

Grouped by part, one block per question or object. This brief went out without a
return format, which is the likeliest reason nothing has come back in a shape
that files — so it is spelled out now.

**For an object** (Parts A, B, D):

```
record:        (the slug this belongs to, e.g. pashupati-seal)
object:        (what it is)
institution:   (who holds it)
accession:     (catalogue or accession number)
dateText:      (as the scholarship states it)
startYear:     (single integer, negative for BCE)
establishedBy: (whose dating this is — a name, not "scholars")
quote:         (verbatim, with page or line reference)
url:           (the page you actually opened)
```

**For a question** (Parts C, E): the question number, the answer, and the source
you answered it from. **A "no" is an answer** — record 05 asked whether a text
calls Seth evil, searched, found that it does not, and that negative changed a
record.

**End with three lists:**

1. **ANSWERED** — with the source for each.
2. **NOT FOUND** — every question you searched and could not settle, **with what
   you searched**. This is not a failure list; it stops the next person
   repeating the search, and it is entered on the records as a finding.
3. **DISAGREEMENTS** — anywhere two scholars gave different answers, with both
   names. The disagreement is more interesting than either answer and the
   dataset has fields for it.

**One field this brief cannot ask you to fill.** The classification fields —
`kundaliniRelation` and `transmissionStatus` — are set in the codebase, not by
you, because they depend on how a record sits beside the others. What is needed
from you is the evidence that decides them, especially for Part C: whether
contact is documented, and separately, whether anything is shown to have
travelled along it. **Those two are not the same question and the collection
keeps them in different fields.**

## What not to do

- Do not design timeline records or assign the five classification labels. That
  needs the codebase open.
- Do not supply a structure the evidence does not have.
- Do not resolve a disagreement between scholars. Send both.
- Do not treat visual similarity as evidence of contact, and do not dismiss a
  correspondence merely because transmission has not been shown. **Both errors
  are equally fatal here**, and the collection exists to hold them apart.
- Do not fill an accession number, seal number or figure reference by inference.
  `NOT FOUND` is better than a plausible one, because a plausible one looks
  checkable.
