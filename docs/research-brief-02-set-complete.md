# Research brief 02 — everything outstanding on the Set / Seth / Sutekh timeline

**For a researcher with web access. Pasteable as-is.**

The dataset is live: 29 records covering Set from Predynastic Egypt to the
present. It was built in an environment that could not reach a single museum
catalogue, archive or scholarly PDF, so it is honest about what it does not
know and thin wherever that honesty bites. This brief is the list of what it
does not know.

Concretely: **22 of the 29 records have no picture at all**, 5 of the 8 pictures
it does have are missing their holding institution, 9 sources have never been
opened, and 38 date claims carry a verification flag.

---

## The standard

This governs everything below. The dataset exists to expose the gap between
what a source says and what gets repeated about it, so a confident paraphrase
damages it more than a gap does.

- **Quote verbatim with page, line or catalogue references.** Do not paraphrase
  and present it as the source.
- **Never reconstruct a transliteration, accession number or figure reference
  from memory.** Quote the published one or write `NOT FOUND`.
- **If you cannot open something, write `NOT FOUND`** rather than answering from
  general knowledge.
- **Give the URL you actually opened** — not one where the information could
  plausibly live.
- **Read each image file's OWN page for its licence.** A Wikimedia category page
  has a licence of its own that has nothing to do with the files in it, and
  every file carries different terms.
- **Never conflate an upload date with an image date**, a Commons page with an
  originating institution, or an engraving of a person with a photograph of one.
- **Mark your inferences as inferences.**

A long `NOT FOUND` list is a good result. A plausible accession number that
turns out wrong is the worst possible result, because it looks checkable.

```
FILENAME:            <exact name, as it appears on the host>
FILE PAGE URL:       <the description page, not the image file>
ORIGINAL FILE URL:   <full size, no width parameter>
ORIGINAL SOURCE URL: <the holding institution's own catalogue record>
INSTITUTION:         <museum / archive / library>
ACCESSION NUMBER:    <or NOT FOUND>
CREATOR:             <photographer / engraver / draughtsman, or unknown>
OBJECT DATE:         <when the DEPICTED THING was made>
PHOTOGRAPH DATE:     <when the photograph or scan was taken>
UPLOAD DATE:         <when the file reached the host — NEVER use as the image date>
LICENCE:             <verbatim, from THIS FILE'S OWN PAGE>
RIGHTS NOTES:        <if the institution states different terms from the host, give BOTH>
DIMENSIONS:          <width x height in pixels>
SUBJECT:             <what the picture is actually OF — which is not always what it is filed under>
IMAGE TYPE:          photograph / engraving / manuscript / relief / modern artwork / diagram
WHAT THE CAPTION SHOULD SAY: <one or two sentences of plain description>
PROVENANCE PROBLEMS: <anything that does not add up — a date that cannot be right, an
                      "own work" claim for a historical photograph, a file categorised
                      under a person or object it cannot depict>
URLS OPENED:
```

**Please actively look for provenance problems.** Real examples found in other
Commons categories: a show bill dated 1886 filed under a man who died in 1806; a
2011 street-art photograph whose filename began "Cardiff1869"; an "own work"
credit dated to the year the subject died. Categories are filing decisions, not
provenance.

## B2. The 400-Year Stela

Object: Egyptian Museum, Cairo; commonly cited JE 60539. 19th Dynasty, under
Ramesses II. Found at Tanis; likely original context Avaris / Pi-Ramesses.

Sources, in order of authority:
- Kitchen, *Ramesside Inscriptions* II pp. 287–288; *RITA* II pp. 116–117
- Sethe, *Der Denkstein mit dem Datum des Jahres 400…*
- Montet, *La stèle de l'an 400 retrouvée*
- Habachi, on the Four Hundred Year Stela and Avaris–Pi-Ramesses
- Breasted, *Ancient Records of Egypt* III §§538–542 *(out of copyright, online)*
- Pritchard, *ANET* p. 252ff — a web copy is at
  `https://www.tarsus.ie/resources/Wisdom-Lit.-/ANET-PDF.pdf`
  **Do not treat this accessible copy as superior to Kitchen, Sethe or Montet.**

Wanted, as complete translated text with the section reference for each part:

1. The **opening royal titulary** of Ramesses II, and the statement that he
   ordered a great granite stela made.
2. The **dating formula** — published renderings give "Year 400, 4th month of
   the third season, day 4" — and the attribution to
   **Seth-the-Great-of-Strength** and **the Ombite**.
3. **Seth's own titulary** on the stela, in royal style.
4. **Seti's full titles.** Published translations include Hereditary Prince,
   Mayor of the City, Vizier, Fan-Bearer on the Right Hand of the King,
   military and foreign-country offices, Royal Scribe, Master of Horse, and —
   the one that matters most here — **High Priest of Seth**. Give the complete
   list from the scholarly edition, not a summary.
5. The **prayer to Seth**. Published renderings include "Hail to thee, O Seth,
   son of Nut, great of strength", "great of strength in the boat of millions of
   years", and Seth "in the bow of the ship of Re". Quote the full passage.
6. The **lunette**: what the inscription calls the deity (published readings give
   "Seth of Ramesses"), the Egyptian, and the preferred transliteration.

**Then, separately, the interpretations.** Do not merge these:

- **Claim A** — the 400-year era commemorates the establishment or major
  institutionalisation of the cult of Seth at Avaris. Who argues this, where.
- **Claim B** — the initiating event remains debated. Who says so, and what the
  competing proposals are.
- **Claim C** — excavation at Avaris independently evidences a Seth temple or
  cult in roughly the relevant Second Intermediate Period window. What was found,
  where published.
- **Flag as obsolete, do not revive**: the older reading of
  "Seth-the-Great-of-Strength / Nubti" as an otherwise unknown Hyksos pharaoh.
  If you find it, report it *as an older interpretation with its date*.

**Why point 5 matters more than it looks.** The dataset already holds visual
evidence of Set spearing Apep from the prow of Ra's barque, and argues from it
that Set was a protective god for most of Egyptian history. If a Ramesside royal
stela independently places Seth "in the bow of the ship of Re" in *text*, that
turns one image into a documented tradition. Quote it exactly. **But do not claim
this stela depicts the Apep battle unless it actually does** — say which.

## B3. Mut el-Kharab, Dakhleh Oasis

This is the evidence that stops Set's decline being told as one nationwide
event, and the dataset currently has it as a signpost with nothing behind it.

Project: `https://www.monash.edu/arts/philosophical-historical-indigenous-studies/dakhleh-oasis-project/excavations/mut-el-kharab`
Publications: same URL + `/publications`

Key reading:
- Colin A. Hope (2016), *Reconstructing the Image of Seth, Lord of the Oasis, in
  his Temple at Mut el-Kharab in Dakhleh Oasis*, pp. 123–145.
  `https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/reconstructing-the-image-of-seth-lord-of-the-oasis-in-his-temple-/`
- Hope & Kaper, *Egyptian Interest in the Oases in the New Kingdom and a New
  Stela for Seth from Mut el-Kharab*
- Hope & Warfe, *The Proscription of Seth Revisited*

**Do not return one generic "Temple of Seth at Dakhleh" summary.** Return a row
per object, in this shape:

```
OBJECT:              <name>
OBJECT NUMBER:       <excavation or registration number>
FINDSPOT:            <trench / area / context>
STRATIGRAPHY:        <phase, and how it was dated>
DATE:                <and the basis for it>
INSCRIPTION:         <hieroglyphic description>
TRANSLITERATION:     <published, or NOT FOUND>
TRANSLATION:         <published, with source>
PUBLICATION:         <author, year, page, FIGURE NUMBER>
EXCAVATOR'S READING: <what the excavators themselves conclude, quoted>
IMAGE:               <per the §A3 block>
```

Wanted at minimum: the earliest Dakhleh evidence for Set; New Kingdom temple
activity; the **Ramesside Seth stela**; **Seth with Nephthys**; the relationship
between Seth and Amun at the site; the **Greater Dakhleh Stela**; the epithet
**"Seth, Lord of the Oasis"**; the **Set-animal determinative** on the Greater
Dakhleh Stela; the **Smaller Dakhleh Stela**; any **change or disappearance of
the determinative** between them; altered Set inscriptions; and separately dated
evidence for Third Intermediate Period, Late Period, Ptolemaic and Roman cult
activity, and for Christianisation.

**Three things to be careful about.**

1. On the determinative change between the Greater and Smaller stelae: report
   **what changed, when, and why the excavators think it changed**. Do not call
   it demonisation — that is the conclusion under test.
2. **Do not infer continuous worship from a temple still standing.** For each
   period, say what physical or textual evidence actually exists. "The temple
   remained in use" needs an object behind it.
3. If Hope & Warfe model the proscription geographically — Thebes, Memphis,
   Delta, Dakhleh, other oases — report that model and its evidence per region.
   The dataset wants to show different trajectories side by side.

# PART D — FIVE QUESTIONS THE DATASET'S OWN CLAIMS DEPEND ON

These are places where the records assert something I cannot currently back.
Answer each explicitly, with a citation. **A "no" answer is as useful as a
"yes" — it tells me to change the record.**

**D1. Does the Scorpion Macehead carry a Set animal on any of its standards?**
The record says this is disputed and names no authority either way. Who says it
does, who says it does not, and on what grounds? Also: the macehead's date is
given in circulation as two different figures — report both and who holds each.

**D2. Is the Seti I / Abydos sign substitution real?**
The record states that in some inscriptions in the Osiris temple at Abydos,
Seti I's name is written with a different sign in place of the Set animal —
described there as reported from secondary knowledge and unchecked. Is it true?
Which inscriptions, photographed where, discussed in which publication?

**D3. Which Sutekh is in the Egyptian–Hittite treaty witness list?**
Egyptian scribes used the Set sign to render Levantine and Anatolian storm gods.
So "Sutekh witnesses the treaty" may mean the Egyptian god, the Hittite storm
god written Egyptian-fashion, or several local Sutekhs listed by place. Give the
witness list verbatim from both the Egyptian and the Akkadian versions, and
report what the specialist literature says about the identification.

**D4. What is the actual chronological range of the Set-spears-Apep motif?**
The record says "attested across much of pharaonic history" and gives no dates,
which is honest but weak. The earliest and latest securely dated examples would
turn a role into a span — which is what the record says it needs.

**D5. What does the 1904 Crowley material actually say about Set?**
Later Thelemic and Typhonian currents make a great deal of Set, and much of that
is twentieth-century development long after Crowley. The dataset deliberately
does not project it back onto 1904 and says it has not checked. So: is Set named
in *Liber AL vel Legis* or the immediately surrounding 1904 material, and if so,
how? Distinguish 1904 from later Typhonian writing, with dates.

# WHAT NOT TO DO

- Do not design timeline records, choose classifications, or decide what counts
  as a precursor versus a parallel. That needs the codebase open.
- Do not reproduce a copyrighted modern translation in bulk. Short quotations
  for comparison, with full bibliographic citation.
- Do not fill an accession number, figure reference or line number by inference.
  `NOT FOUND` is better than a plausible one.
- Do not attach an image to a record because it is *about Set*. Attach it
  because it is the object that record is about.
- Do not resolve a disagreement. If two scholars differ, send both.
